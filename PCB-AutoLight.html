Automatic Night LED PCB

Input

9V Battery

Control

Light Dependent Resistor (LDR)

Output

LED

#1

Why I built this

I wanted to go deeper into PCB design and understand more components beyond an LED and a resistor. This project is a starting point for something more useful too, a simple automatic device that reacts to its environment but can still be overridden by hand

Along the way I learned to use a comparator and a transistor, and how they hand off a signal from one stage to the next instead of one component doing everything alone.

Drag to rotate the 3D model. Scroll to zoom.

#2

How it works

The LDR's resistance drops in light and climbs in darkness, shifting the voltage at one spot in the circuit. The LM393 chip watches that spot against a line you set with the knob, RV1, and constantly checks: above the line, or below it? Cross it, and the chip flips its output from off to on.

That output can't drive the LED directly, its too weak. It opens a valve instead: Q1, the transistor, where a small trickle at its base swings open a much bigger flow from +9V to ground. Open the valve and the LED gets full current. Closed, it gets none. No in-between, no fade — just a switch flipping the instant the room crosses your line..

#4

Steps to build

  1. 1. Wire the sensor divider, +9V through R4 (the LDR) to a node, then through R1 (10kΩ) to ground, with the node feeding U1 pin 2 (IN-).
  2. 2. Wire the threshold pot: +9V to RV1 pin 1, ground to pin 3, and the wiper (pin 2) to U1 pin 3 (IN+).
  3. 3. Power the comparator by connecting pin 8 to +9V, pin 4 to ground, with C2 (100nF) bridging the two right at the chip.
  4. 4. Add the pull-up, R3 (4.7kΩ) from +9V to U1 pin 1 (OUT), since the comparator's output is open-collector.
  5. 5. Drive the transistor base,R5 (4.7kΩ) from OUT to Q1's base, sized for roughly 0.9mA to saturate it.
  6. 6. Wire the LED switch with +9V through R2 (330Ω) through the LED to Q1's collector, with the emitter to ground.
  7. 7. Terminate the unused half of the comparator (U1B) by no-connecting its pins so ERC doesn't flag them.
  8. 8. Add bulk power filtering: C1 (100µF) across +9V/ground near the input connector.
  9. 9. Assign footprints: DIP-8 for the comparator, TO-92 for the transistor, 5mm for the LDR, and match the pot's footprint to the part you're sourcing.
  10. 10. Place and route: pot near a board edge, C2 tight against the comparator, LDR clear of the LED's light path, ground poured as a zone, then run ERC and DRC and generate the manufacturing files.
